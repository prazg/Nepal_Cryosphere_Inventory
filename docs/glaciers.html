Nepal glacier inventory

4,480 glaciers, 4,215 km² of ice inside Nepal's borders, with annual surface speed to 2025. Outlines are fixed; the velocity layer refreshes as ITS_LIVE republishes.

Where the ice sits

Glacier area per 50 m elevation band. Nepal's ice is concentrated between 5,000 and 6,200 m — above the summer freezing level for much of the year, but with a long thin tail of debris-covered tongues reaching below 4,000 m. Those tongues are where the outburst-flood lakes form.

What this data cannot tell you

There is no usable velocity trend here

Comparing 2000–2009 against 2016–2024 gives a median slowdown of about 46%, and 96% of glaciers appear to slow. That result is an artefact and should not be published.

Speed is computed as the hypotenuse of two velocity components, so it cannot go below zero and random error pushes it upward. Early years rest on a handful of image pairs; recent years rest on thousands. As the error collapsed, apparent speed fell with it. Median signal-to-noise across Nepal is 1.59 in the 2000s against 12.08 today — the older velocities sit at the noise floor.

Filtering for high early signal-to-noise makes it worse, not better: it selects the glaciers noise happened to inflate, so regression to the mean manufactures the same slowdown. The field velocity_change_interpretable is set to false for every glacier in this inventory.

Read this before using the data

Outlines are 20 years old

RGI 7.0 source imagery for Nepal spans 1992–2010, median 1999. Sampling 2024 velocity inside a 1999 outline includes ground that has since deglaciated. The inventory is not a current map of ice extent.

Terminus type is unassigned

Every glacier in RGI region 15 carries term_type = 9. Lake-terminating glaciers cannot be identified from RGI alone, which matters directly for outburst-flood work. Pair with a dedicated lake inventory.

Two definitions of "in Nepal"

4,679 glaciers intersect the border; 4,480 have their representative point inside it. Figures above use the second. The first pulls in Tibetan glaciers such as Rongbuk that are only 2% inside Nepal. Both are in the file, with frac_within_nepal.

The border itself is contested

The Kalapani–Limpiyadhura area in the northwest is disputed between Nepal and India. This build follows the geoBoundaries gbOpen ADM0 line. A different boundary changes the far-western counts.
