Nepal glacial lakes

1,429 lakes covering 81.04 km², mapped for 2022 and matched to the 201 Nepali glaciers they touch. Built to sit alongside the glacier inventory, and to fill the gap RGI 7.0 leaves where terminus type should be.

Why this exists

RGI 7.0 records term_type = 9 — unassigned — for every glacier in region 15, so the glacier inventory alone cannot say which Nepali glaciers end in water. Matching mapped lakes back to glacier outlines answers that question directly: 201 glaciers, holding 886 km² of ice, have a lake at their mapped margin.

Lakes touching ice are the ones growing

Share of lakes that expanded between 2016–17 and 2022–24, grouped by distance to the nearest mapped glacier. Lakes at the ice margin expand roughly three times as often as detached ones. Across all 918 assessable lakes the net area gain is 5.1% — close to the 5.1% figure the source study reports for High Mountain Asia as a whole, which is a useful independent check that the Nepal subset behaves like the parent dataset.

Expansion means a gain above both 0.005 km² and the source study's area uncertainty. Lakes under 0.01 km², or with fewer than three cloud-free images in either period, are not assessed.

Two decades of glacier retreat, made visible

216 lakes now lie inside outlines that RGI maps as ice

RGI 7.0's Nepal outlines come from imagery with a median date of 1999. The lakes are from 2022. So 216 lakes — 23.73 km² of open water, across 163 glaciers — fall inside polygons the glacier inventory still calls glacier. Hi-MAG independently classifies 98 of them as proglacial and only 16 as supraglacial, so these are overwhelmingly not ponds on the ice surface. They are lakes filling ground the terminus has vacated.

This cuts both ways. It is a real, measurable retreat signal. It also means every "ice contact" judgement in this inventory is made against a 1999 margin and will overstate contact for glaciers that have since pulled back. Treat dist_to_rgi1999_m as a distance to a two-decade-old margin, not to today's ice. Confirming present-day contact needs current outlines, which RGI does not provide.

Do lakes make their glaciers flow faster?

Elsewhere, lake-terminating glaciers often flow faster than land-terminating ones. This data does not show that for Nepal. Comparing raw medians suggests contact-lake glaciers are faster — 2.11 against 1.81 m/yr — but that reverses once you compare like with like, because glaciers with lakes are systematically larger.

What this is not

Not a hazard ranking

There is no danger score here, deliberately. Judging whether a lake is likely to burst needs moraine dam geometry, freeboard, ice-core presence in the dam, slope stability above the lake, and downstream exposure. None of that is in these sources. The inventory supplies the objective ingredients — area, growth, ice contact, elevation, parent glacier, flood history — and stops there. A composite score built from these fields alone would look authoritative and mean very little.

Not a complete census of small lakes

The source study's own accuracy assessment reports detection near 96% for lakes of 20,000–100,000 m² and all lakes above 100,000 m², but roughly 25% for the smallest class below 5,400 m². Small lakes are under-counted here, and small lakes have caused destructive floods. Do not read the count of tiny lakes as complete.

Not seasonally resolved

Extents come from summer composites chosen to catch lakes at maximum extent. Supraglacial ponds that fill and drain within a season are poorly represented by any single number.
